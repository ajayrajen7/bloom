## How to design items and targets

**Step 1 — Design your targets first.**

Targets are category containers, not item-specific containers. A target must represent a category that ALL items assigned to it genuinely belong to. Choose a sorting principle (colour, shape, type, size) and name targets after that principle — not after a specific item inside them.

When item count > target count, multiple items share a target. That target MUST be a genuine category for all its members.

**Step 2 — Select items that fit your targets.**

Choose items from the available sprites. Every item must unambiguously belong to its assigned target. A child should be able to explain the match: "It's red, so it goes in the red basket."

When multiple items share one target, each item must be a **different object** — different name, different shape or animal or thing. Do NOT use two sprite variations of the same object (`apple.png` and `red-apple.png` are both apples — that is one object, not two). Items sharing a target must offer a real discrimination challenge: a child should clearly see them as different things that share a category.

**Worked example — 3 items, 2 targets, theme: fruits and baskets**

WRONG (category error):
- targets: apple_basket ("Apple Basket"), banana_basket ("Banana Basket")
- items: apple → apple_basket, orange → apple_basket ← orange does not belong in the "Apple Basket". Lie to the child.

WRONG (same-object variants):
- targets: red_basket ("Red Basket"), yellow_basket ("Yellow Basket")
- items: apple → red_basket, red-apple → red_basket, banana → yellow_basket ← apple and red-apple are the same object. One decision, not two.

CORRECT:
- targets: round_basket ("Round Basket"), long_basket ("Long Basket")
- items: apple → round_basket, orange → round_basket, banana → long_basket
- Apple and orange are genuinely different objects, both round. Banana is distinct. Three real decisions.

**Critical rule:** Never name a target after a single specific item (e.g. "Apple Basket", "Cat House") if multiple items must share that target. Use category names (colour, shape, type, size) instead.

## Rules

1. Every item's targetId must exactly match an id in the targets array.
2. Target labels must be distinct. A child must tell targets apart by label alone.
3. Item-to-target mappings must be logically consistent. The sorting principle must hold for every item-target pair. No exceptions.
4. When itemCount > targetCount, targets MUST be category containers (colour, type, shape) — never item-specific containers.
5. Item labels must be single common words a 2-year-old knows (apple, dog, cat, ball — not pomegranate, dalmatian, loafer). Item labels must be unique — no two items may share the same label. Target labels describe the category container; two words are acceptable if they describe what the basket holds (e.g. "Round Basket", "Big Basket").
6. Prompt text: single imperative sentence, under 10 words, action verb (put, find, help, give, match), child-directed.
7. Asset refs must come ONLY from the available sprites list above.
8. Output only valid JSON. Nothing else.
